Looking for the catalogue? Go to the [official International Dunhuang Programme website](https://idp.bl.uk/) and choose **Search**. Dunhuang Within is an independent guide to the collection.

## What can you find in the IDP catalogue?

You can find records for manuscripts, paintings, prints and other objects from sites along the Eastern Silk Roads. Many records include digital images. To browse by example, start with the [IDP’s Cave 17 collection guide](https://idp.bl.uk/discover/learning/dunhuang/collection-items/cave-17-the-library-cave/). If you already know an item’s shelfmark, enter it in the search box. The [IDP search guide](https://idp.bl.uk/using-the-collection/) explains how to filter records by language, holding institution or image availability.

For the history of the chamber and how its contents came to be held in several countries, read our [Mogao Cave 17 guide](/places/dunhuang/cave-17/).

## How the programme began

Representatives of major collection-holding institutions met in 1993, and the collaboration took the name International Dunhuang Project in 1994. It is now called the International Dunhuang Programme; both names use the abbreviation IDP. Its work has involved shared cataloguing, conservation knowledge, research, and digital access.

## Why it matters

Digitization does not physically reunite the archive or erase contested collection histories. It does allow manuscripts, paintings, and records held in different places to be studied within a connected research environment.

Read the [Dunhuang manuscripts guide](/places/dunhuang/manuscripts/) for examples of what the texts contain and how to identify an item by shelfmark.
